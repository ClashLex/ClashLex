# Prompt: raise the ANSIL raid to bulletchicken level

Paste the prompt below into a fresh session together with `make_raid.py` (ours), bulletchicken's `make_fight.py`, and the latest storyboard. It makes the model write a beat sheet around the letters A, N, S, I, L first, then build the animation act by act.

## The prompt

```text
ROLE
You are a senior 2D animator and Python engineer. Raise an animated GitHub profile banner (pure SMIL SVG, generated by one Python file) to the level of a reference file. Ours is competent but flat. The reference is a short film.

INPUTS
1. make_raid.py: our generator. A pirate and a navy officer fight on the ANSI Shadow letters of ANSIL. 31 s loop.
2. make_fight.py: the reference (bulletchicken). Read ALL of it before planning. Learn its craft. Do not copy its characters, text, jokes or code.
3. Storyboard PNGs of our current output.

HARD CONSTRAINTS
- Pure SMIL SVG shown through <img> in a GitHub README: no JavaScript, no CSS animation, no external files, no filters on animated elements, no foreignObject. Light and dark themes from a palette dict.
- One Python file, standard library plus optional Pillow. Flags: --preview (PNG frames via Playwright) and --out.
- Original characters and props only. Nothing taken from an existing franchise: no names, designs, logos or props.
- Each SVG at most 750 KB, valid XML, at most 60 elements animating at the same moment, and the first frame must equal the last frame (no loop seam).
- The banner stays ANSIL as vector ASCII art (ART_RAW, LETTER_COLS), editable by the user.
- Tone: dry and human. No hype, no cute faces, no pop-up labels, no corporate language.

WHAT MAKES THE REFERENCE GOOD (each point is a requirement)
1. The page is the set and the jokes come from its content. Every gag must only work because the banner says ANSIL. Use the letter shapes as terrain (A apex, N staircase, S shelves, I pillar or mast, L ledge).
2. A timestamped beat sheet: 20 to 24 beats in 32 to 36 s. Each beat is a setup plus a payoff. Callbacks: an object introduced early is used again later.
3. The world escalates in acts. The page itself transforms (floor cracks, a hole, a camera move to a new place) and is then healed.
4. Acting: anticipation holds, follow-through, smears only when travel exceeds body length, hit-stop, secondary motion (hat, coat, rope, cloth lag), head turns, double-takes, shrugs. At least 60 named poses.
5. Precision from geometry: blade-crossing points decide where a cut lands, footfalls decide which letter dips, occlusion tricks (per-limb opacity, clip to a hole or to page-minus-hole) for climbing in and out of things.
6. One visual language: ink lines, one accent colour per character, thin effects (rings, forks, glints). No big filled flashes, starbursts or glow.
7. Code craft: timestamped beat comments, one small helper per effect, deterministic output, no dead code.

PROCESS (do not skip steps; stop where it says STOP)
Step 1. Read and report on one screen: the 10 techniques from the reference you will adopt (with line numbers) and the 10 biggest weaknesses of ours.
Step 2. Beat sheet as a table: time, beat, ANSIL-specific mechanism, callback, effects. Start from the proposed acts below and improve them. STOP and show it. Wait for approval.
Step 3. Engine upgrades before choreography: surface map built from ART_RAW (walkable tops, a letter dips on each footfall), camera track (pan, punch-in, shake), pose library to 60+, secondary-motion helper (rope, flag, hat), reverse-play of the fragment simulation for the heal, glyph reuse through <defs> and <use> to cut file size, and a lint step (tracks monotonic, concurrent animation count, size, loop seam).
Step 4. Build act by act. After each act render a 12 fps contact sheet in light and dark. Review it as a harsh editor (list 5 problems), fix them, then continue.
Step 5. Final QA against the checklist, full contact sheet, GIF and MP4 previews, zip, and a short README with the beat sheet.

PROPOSED ACTS (improve, do not shrink)
I Setup 0-6 s: ripple boom runs A to L. Pirate rope-swings in, boots the A apex, lands on the L ledge. Climbs the I and ties a black flag to it. Pins the bounty poster to the S middle bar with a dagger.
II Chase 6-12 s: officer slides down a rope onto the N staircase. Compares poster and pirate (look, look, spyglass snap). Pirate whistles, then bolts across the S shelves; the officer's slashes cut each shelf where the blade crosses. Pirate swings on the flag rope; an air-slash cuts the flag and it lands on the officer's head. Blinded, he swings wild and fells the I like a tree.
III The page breaks 12-22 s: the I's crash cracks the floor line (ink cracks grow). A hole opens (page-minus-hole clip). Letters tilt toward it, both fall, camera pans down to a below-deck set (barrels, a swinging lantern). Sword stuck in a barrel, balance gag on a rolling barrel, fuse line sparks along a path, chain of barrel explosions with a camera punch-in on each.
IV Blast and heal 22-34 s: blast throws both up through the hole, camera pans back, they hit the banner from below and it shatters outward by impact. Rubble falls, then the fragments play their physics in reverse and click back into place A, N, S, I, L, each with a small ring. Closing gag: the officer's hand sticks out of the L waving a white handkerchief. The last frame equals the first.

ACCEPTANCE CHECKLIST
- 20 to 24 beats, no more than 1 s without a new action, 6 or more callbacks.
- Every cut, break and dip position comes from geometry, not typed coordinates.
- Ink plus one accent per character in both themes.
- Valid XML, no scripts, at most 750 KB each, seam test passes, works as <img> in Chromium.
- Contact sheet per act, plus GIF and MP4 preview, plus zip.
```

## Choreography gaps and the change for each

The engine is not the problem. Each row is a gap in how the engine is used, and what to do about it.

| Gap in ours | What the reference does | Change for ANSIL |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The story could run on any logo | Gags depend on the words on the page | Letters become terrain and props: A apex, N staircase, S shelves, I mast or pillar, L ledge |
| One idea (duel, cannon, cannon) stretched over 31 s | About 25 beats in 31 s, each a setup and a payoff | 20 to 24 beats; every object introduced early (rope, flag, dagger, poster, sword) comes back |
| Flat world: floor and banner never change | Cracks, a hole, a climb, a void, then a heal | Four acts: set, chase, the page breaks and the scene moves below deck, blast and heal |
| Figures fight but do not act | Whistles, shrugs, hips, stuck-sword tugging | Spyglass compare, whistle then bolt, flag over the head, white handkerchief ending |
| Damage is staged for spectacle | Damage has a cause and changes what comes next | The officer, blinded by the flag, fells the I; the fallen I cracks the floor and opens the hole |
| Dead time while debris sits | Actions overlap; something always moves | No more than 1 s without a new action; the next beat starts inside the previous payoff |
| No camera | Screen shake and hit-stop only | Add a camera track: pan down into the hole and back up, punch-in on each explosion |
| Loud effects (yellow starbursts, big flashes) | Thin ink rings, forks and glints | Ink-only effects, one accent colour per character |
| Generic tide wipe resets the page | The reset is part of the story (heal) | Reverse-play the fragment physics so the letters click back A, N, S, I, L |
| Props are static | Stuck katana, bear, windwall have their own motion | Flag and rope with lag, swinging lantern, fuse spark running along a path |
| Pose-swap fights, about 25 poses | About 90 poses | 60 or more named poses, with anticipation holds and follow-through |
| 820 KB because every fragment copies its letter | 700 KB for a much bigger film | Define each glyph once in defs and reuse it with use plus a clip per fragment |

## Engine additions needed first

1. Surface map from `ART_RAW`: which cells can be stood on, so figures snap to letter tops and each footfall dips the letter below.
2. Camera track: pan, punch-in and shake on one group, driven from the same timeline.
3. Secondary motion: a lagging follower track for hat, flag, rope and coat tails.
4. Reverse-play: run the existing fragment simulation backwards for the heal.
5. Glyph reuse: one `<defs>` entry per letter and a `<use>` per fragment, to bring the file under 750 KB.
6. Lint step: tracks monotonic, at most 60 elements animating at once, size budget, loop seam test, a contact sheet for every act.
